# Formal Proposal: Adoption of the BOSC Community Library
## Submitted to the Ministry of Education

**Date:** May 2026  
**From:** BOSC Community Library Maintainers  
**Reference:** Open Educational Resources — Digital Infrastructure Proposal

## Executive Summary

The Ministry of Education is invited to formally adopt the **BOSC Community Library** as the national open educational resource (OER) platform for tertiary and secondary institutions. This proposal demonstrates that adopting BOSC will deliver significant cost savings through reduced Total Cost of Ownership (TCO), eliminate vendor lock-in risk from proprietary platforms, and position Uganda as a regional leader in open digital education.

---

## 1. The Problem: Proprietary Platforms and Their Hidden Costs

Across Africa, educational institutions are increasingly dependent on proprietary learning management systems (LMS) and content platforms — products such as Blackboard, Cengage MindTap, and Pearson digital libraries. These platforms impose a structural problem that the Ministry must address proactively.

### 1.1 Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)

Proprietary platforms do not disclose their full cost in their initial licensing fee. A complete TCO analysis must include:

| Cost Component | Proprietary Platform (est./year) | BOSC Community Library |
|----------------|----------------------------------|------------------------|
| Licensing fees | $40,000 – $120,000 | $0 |
| Per-user fees | $15 – $50/student/year | $0 |
| Customization/integration | $10,000 – $50,000 | Community-contributed |
| Training | $5,000 – $20,000 | Open documentation |
| Data export/migration | $5,000 – $30,000 | Standard formats (always) |
| Annual renewal increase | 5 – 15% per year | Stable (open) |
| **Estimated 5-Year TCO** | **$350,000 – $1.2M** | **< $50,000** |

*Estimates based on published pricing from Blackboard and Cengage for African institutional licenses.*

The Ministry's budget devoted to recurring proprietary licensing fees could instead fund teacher training, hardware procurement, or rural connectivity — investments with lasting impact.

### 1.2 Vendor Lock-In

Vendor lock-in occurs when an institution becomes so dependent on a single provider's proprietary formats, APIs, and workflows that switching becomes prohibitively expensive — even when the vendor raises prices or degrades service quality. Symptoms include:

- Student and course data stored in proprietary formats that cannot be easily exported.
- Teaching staff trained exclusively on one vendor's interface.
- Contractual clauses that restrict data portability.
- Termination of service or product discontinuation (e.g., when a company is acquired or exits a market).

The BOSC Community Library eliminates lock-in entirely:

- All content is stored in open, portable formats (Markdown, JSON, HTML).
- The codebase and all data are available under Apache License 2.0 — the Ministry could fork and self-host at any time.
- No single company controls the platform's future.

## 2. The Solution: BOSC Community Library

### 2.1 What BOSC Provides

- A curated index of verified, freely licensed educational resources across all major subjects.
- Localized content in English, French, Swahili, and Arabic — serving Uganda's multilingual educational context.
- A searchable JSON resource database enabling integration with existing Ministry portals.
- A community governance model ensuring content remains accurate, current, and relevant.
- Full transparency: every change is publicly recorded in version control.

### 2.2 Alignment With National Policy

The adoption of BOSC aligns directly with:

- **Uganda's National ICT Policy** — promoting open standards and digital sovereignty.
- **African Union's Digital Transformation Strategy 2020-2030** — supporting open educational content.
- **UNESCO's OER Recommendation (2019)** — which calls on member states to prioritize open educational resources in public institutions.

## 4. Risk Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| Platform abandonment | Apache 2.0 license: Ministry can self-host at any time |
| Content quality | Peer-review workflow built into contribution process |
| Language gaps | Active localization program (Swahili, French pages live) |
| Technical capacity | Full documentation; LibreOffice and standard web stack |

## 5. Conclusion and Request

The Ministry of Education is respectfully requested to:

1. **Formally endorse** the BOSC Community Library as a recommended national OER platform.
2. **Allocate pilot funding** of $20,000 for the 3-month institutional pilot (Phase 1).
3. **Designate a liaison** within the Ministry to participate in BOSC's governance council.

Adopting BOSC is not merely a cost decision. It is a declaration of educational sovereignty — that Uganda's students deserve access to world-class learning resources without dependence on foreign proprietary vendors whose interests may not align with national development goals.

We welcome the opportunity to present this proposal in person at the Ministry's convenience.
